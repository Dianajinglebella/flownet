1. Abstract
Social media was supposed to connect us. Instead, it trapped creators inside walled gardens, strip-mined
user attention for ad revenue, and hid the machinery of recommendation behind opaque,
engagement-maximizing algorithms. The result: a creator economy worth $249 billion in 2026, projected to
exceed $1 trillion by 2033 (Coherent Market Insights) — built on rented land, where a single algorithmic
tweak can erase a livelihood overnight. 78% of creators report burnout; 75% feel compelled to post
constantly to appease opaque algorithms; 37% are considering leaving entirely (Patreon, Billion Dollar
Boy, 2025).
Flownet proposes a fundamentally different architecture. By combining user-controlled AI
recommendation with portable, on-chain creator identity and an open algorithmic marketplace,
Flownet makes the feed a tool the user wields — not a weapon wielded against them. Creators keep their
audience relationships as cryptographically signed social graphs, immune to platform lock-in. And the
economics flip: revenue flows from genuine value exchange — tipping, token-gated access, transparent
sponsored placements — rather than from harvesting eyeball-hours.
The timing is right. The SocialFi sector grew 300% year-over-year to $5 billion in 2025 (Chainalysis).
Bluesky proved mainstream appetite for protocol-based social with 40 million users. Farcaster and Lens
have validated the on-chain identity thesis — but none has delivered the full stack of usable AI, portable
identity, and a working creator economy in a single product.
This whitepaper outlines Flownet's thesis, its hybrid on-chain/off-chain architecture, the AI-native feed
pipeline, and a three-phase roadmap from MVP to scale. It includes a detailed comparison with the AT
Protocol (Kleppmann et al., 2024) — the most rigorous decentralized social protocol to date — and
explains where Flownet extends beyond it. It is written for builders, investors, and anyone who believes
the social internet deserves a second chance.
2. The Problem: Broken Creator Economics
Meet Ava. She's a food photographer with 180,000 followers on Instagram. Three years of daily posts,
recipe collaborations, and community building. Then, one Tuesday morning, her reach drops 70%. No
warning, no explanation. The algorithm changed. Ava's story is not unique — it is the universal experience
of the modern creator.
2.1  Platform Lock-In
Today's social platforms hold creators hostage through a simple mechanism: the platform owns the
relationship between a creator and their audience. A follower on Instagram is not the creator's follower —
it is Instagram's user, shown the creator's content at Instagram's discretion. When a creator moves to a
new platform, they start from zero. Their audience, their reputation, their content history — all left behind.

2.2  Opaque, Adversarial Algorithms
The recommendation engine is the most powerful editorial force in human history, yet it operates in total
darkness. Users cannot see why they are shown what they are shown. They cannot tune, swap, or disable
the algorithm. The feed is optimized for a single metric — time-on-platform — which systematically favors
outrage, sensationalism, and addictive loops over substance and serendipity.
For creators, this means their work is not judged by its quality or relevance but by its ability to trigger
engagement. A thoughtful essay loses to a screenshot of a hot take. A ten-minute tutorial loses to a
fifteen-second clip. The algorithm does not reward craft; it rewards compliance with its invisible incentives.
2.3  Extractive Monetization
The dominant business model of social media — surveillance advertising — creates a three-way
misalignment. Users want relevance and privacy. Creators want fair compensation and audience access.
Advertisers want targeting precision. The platform, caught in the middle, resolves every conflict in favor of
ad revenue. Users are profiled. Creators are throttled unless they pay to boost. And the value a creator
generates flows overwhelmingly to the platform, not to the creator.
The Web3 social movement — Farcaster, Lens Protocol, Steemit, Friend.tech — has articulated these
problems clearly. But most solutions have optimized for decentralization as an end in itself, producing
technically impressive protocols that remain unusable for mainstream creators. The missing piece is not
more blockchain; it is better AI — AI that works for the user.
2.4  Competitive Landscape
Several projects have attempted to fix social media's broken incentives. Each has made meaningful
progress on a subset of the problem — but none has delivered the full stack: usable AI, portable identity,
and a working creator economy.

2.5  Bluesky & the AT Protocol: A Deeper Look
Bluesky deserves special attention as the most successful decentralized social project to date
(Kleppmann et al., 2024). Built on the Authenticated Transfer Protocol (AT Protocol), Bluesky
introduces several innovations that inform — and constrain — the design space Flownet operates in:
 Architecture: AT Protocol separates infrastructure into five components — Personal Data Servers
(PDS) for hosting, Relays for firehose aggregation, App Views for indexing, Feed Generators for
custom algorithms, and Labelers for moderation. Each layer can have multiple competing providers.
This modularity is a genuine advance over monolithic platforms.
 Identity: Users are identified by immutable DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) with DNS-based handles
for readability. The did:plc method uses self-certifying operation logs with rotatable keys — meaning
users can change hosts without losing their identity or social graph. This is the gold standard for
decentralized identity.

 Data model: User data lives in signed repositories organized as Merkle Search Trees — a balanced,
content-addressed structure that allows efficient proofs of inclusion and exclusion. Records use
DAG-CBOR encoding and conform to schemas called Lexicons, enabling multiple social apps on
shared infrastructure.
 Moderation philosophy: AT Protocol separates "speech" (the permissive data layer where anyone
can publish) from "reach" (the opinionated discovery layer where labelers and feed generators apply
judgments). This is architecturally elegant and avoids the false dichotomy of "censorship vs. free
speech."
Where AT Protocol falls short — and where Flownet extends:
 No AI-native feed layer: AT Protocol's Feed Generators return post IDs selected by arbitrary criteria,
but there is no built-in embedding pipeline, no explainability, and no user-facing algorithm
marketplace. Tens of thousands of custom feeds exist, but they are opaque selection rules — not
tunable, explainable AI. Flownet's six-stage recommendation pipeline with visible explanation tags is a
qualitative leap.
 No creator economy: AT Protocol has no monetization layer. Bluesky reached a $700M valuation
with zero revenue, and the protocol specification contains no mechanism for creator payments,
tipping, token-gated content, or sponsored placements. Flownet embeds the economic layer at the
protocol level.
 Centralized indexing costs: A full replica of all AT Protocol repositories can be maintained for
~$153/month (as of mid-2024 with ~6M users), but running an App View with summary indexes is
substantially more expensive. In practice, Bluesky Social PBC runs nearly all indexing infrastructure.
Flownet's hybrid architecture avoids this bottleneck by keeping the canonical identity layer on-chain
while running content indexing as a standard service — no full-network relay required.
 Public data trade-off: AT Protocol posts, likes, follows, and blocks are all public by design. This
enables auditing but limits privacy. Flownet adds client-side embeddings and zk-SNARK proofs to
preserve user privacy while maintaining verifiability.
 No blockchain integration: AT Protocol deliberately avoids cryptocurrency for identity and naming,
which simplifies onboarding but forecloses on-chain economic primitives like bonding-curve tokens
and trustless tipping.
Flownet's relationship with AT Protocol is complementary, not adversarial. The roadmap includes AT
Protocol bridge support (Section 10), enabling cross-posting and social-graph import between Flownet
and Bluesky — extending AT Protocol's reach layer with AI recommendation and economic infrastructure
it currently lacks.

4. How It Works: A User's Journey
To make the architecture tangible, consider two stories — one user, one creator.
4.1  Mika Discovers Flownet
Mika signs up with her email. Optionally, she connects a wallet — but she doesn't need to. The
onboarding wizard asks three questions about her interests, then offers to import her existing follows from
Twitter via the social-graph bridge. Within thirty seconds, she has a personalized feed.
Below each post, Mika sees a small tag: "Recommended because you follow food creators and this post is
trending in food photography." She taps the tag and opens the algorithm panel. She's currently using
"Serendipity", a community-built algorithm that blends topic relevance with randomness. She can switch
to "Deep Focus" (long-form only), "Local Pulse" (location-weighted), or build her own. Each algorithm's
logic is published and auditable.

Ava — our food photographer — creates a Flownet creator profile. She mints her creator identity on Base
(L2 Ethereum), which records her follower relationships in a portable social graph via Ceramic and
ComposeDB. If Flownet ever shuts down, Ava can point her graph at any compatible client and her
180,000 followers come with her.
She enables creator tokens — a bonding-curve ERC-20 that lets superfans buy into her success. Token
holders unlock exclusive content, early access, and voting on her next recipe series. Ava earns from direct
tipping, token appreciation, and transparent sponsored placements — partnerships where both the
creator and the audience can see the sponsor, the terms, and the payment.

5.2  Design Principles
Six principles guide every architectural decision in Flownet:
 Progressive decentralization: Start with a usable product, decentralize incrementally. Email-first
signup today; full self-custody as an upgrade path.
 Credible exit: Users must be able to leave Flownet and take their social graph, content hashes, and
token balances with them. The on-chain layer guarantees this.
 AI as user agent: The recommendation system works for the user, not the platform. No
engagement-maximizing dark patterns. The user's algorithm choice is sovereign.
 Minimal on-chain footprint: Put only what must be trustless on-chain. Everything else runs on
optimized off-chain infrastructure for speed and cost.
 Open by default: All algorithms, moderation policies, and ranking logic are open-source and
auditable. Trust is verified, not assumed.
 Creator-aligned economics: Revenue flows through, not from, creators. The platform's incentive is
to maximize creator revenue, not ad impressions.
5.3  Hybrid Architecture Rationale

Fully on-chain social networks face an inescapable trilemma: cost, speed, and UX. Posting a message
on-chain costs gas; reading a feed requires indexing the entire chain; and wallet-first onboarding alienates
99% of potential users. Flownet resolves this by putting only what must be trustless on-chain: identity,
follow relationships, token balances, and content hashes. Everything else — feed ranking, media storage,
real-time notifications — runs on optimized off-chain infrastructure.
This is a deliberate contrast with AT Protocol's approach. In AT Protocol, all user data lives in signed
repositories hosted on Personal Data Servers, and a Relay aggregates updates into a public firehose that
App Views index. This architecture scales the data layer but creates an expensive indexing bottleneck: a
full-network relay grows with every user's every action. Flownet avoids this by keeping only the canonical
ownership records (identity, follows, tokens) on-chain, while content lives in a standard database with
content hashes anchored to IPFS/Arweave for permanence. The result: sub-200ms feed latency without a
full-network indexer.
The bridge between the two layers is the Ceramic social graph. When a user follows a creator, the follow
is recorded in ComposeDB (a decentralized document store anchored to the Ethereum mainnet via
Ceramic streams). The Flownet API indexes these streams for fast querying, but the canonical record
lives on the decentralized layer — giving users credible exit. Unlike AT Protocol's did:plc, which relies on a
centralized PLC directory (with a transparency log planned but not yet deployed), Ceramic DIDs are
anchored to Ethereum's mainnet — providing stronger decentralization guarantees from day one, at the
cost of slightly higher write latency for identity operations.
5.4  Smart Contracts
Three core contracts, all deployed on Base L2 for low gas costs:
 CreatorToken.sol — ERC-20 with a polynomial bonding curve. Creators mint their token; price rises
with supply, aligning early supporters with creator growth. 5% of each trade goes to the creator; 2% to
the protocol treasury.
 TipJar.sol — Gasless tipping via meta-transactions (EIP-2771). Users tip in ETH or stablecoins; the
creator receives 95%, the protocol takes 5%.
 AccessPass.sol — ERC-721 NFTs for gated content. Creators define tiers; holding the NFT unlocks
content in the client. Transferable, so fans can trade access.
5.5  Security & Privacy Architecture
Flownet treats privacy as a structural guarantee, not a policy promise. The architecture enforces data
minimization at every layer:
 Zero-knowledge proofs for reputation: Creators can prove follower-count thresholds or
engagement metrics to sponsors without revealing their full audience data, using zk-SNARKs over
their Ceramic social graph.

 Client-side embedding: User preference vectors are computed on-device and sent to the

 End-to-end encryption for DMs: All direct messages use the Signal protocol (Double Ratchet +

Open-source algorithms and on-chain identity records make this verification practical.

6.3  Explainability
Every post in the feed carries a visible explanation tag. This is not optional, not hidden behind a settings
menu — it is a first-class UI element. Explanations build trust and give users the feedback they need to
tune their algorithm selection. They also create a natural accountability mechanism: an algorithm that
produces poor explanations will be replaced by users.
Explanations are generated at two levels of detail: a summary tag (one line, always visible — e.g.,
"Trending in food photography + followed by 3 people you follow") and a deep-dive panel (opened on tap,
showing the full scoring breakdown: topic match score, social proximity score, freshness score, diversity
bonus, and the algorithm's own weighting rationale). This two-level design balances glanceability with
auditability.
6.4  Content Moderation: Speech vs. Reach
Flownet adopts the "speech vs. reach" framework pioneered by AT Protocol (Kleppmann et al., 2024)
and extends it with AI enforcement. The principle: the speech layer (data storage, identity, and federation)
remains permissive — anyone can publish. The reach layer (discovery, ranking, and recommendation) is
opinionated — content must pass moderation before the AI pipeline can amplify it. This separation avoids
the false dichotomy of "censorship vs. free speech" by distinguishing between the right to publish and the
privilege of algorithmic distribution.
Within this framework, Flownet's moderation operates as a three-layer defense:
 Layer 1 — Automated classifiers: A fine-tuned BERT model screens every post at ingest time for
policy violations (hate speech, harassment, CSAM, spam). Content flagged with high confidence
(>0.95) is automatically removed; borderline content (0.7-0.95) is routed to human review. Processing
adds < 8ms per post.
 Layer 2 — Community reporting: Users can flag content with structured reasons. Reports are
weighted by reporter reputation (long-standing users with low false-report rates have higher weight).
Three weighted reports trigger automatic review.
 Layer 3 — Human appeals: A trained moderation team reviews escalated content and appeals.
Decisions are logged and published in aggregate (monthly transparency reports) without identifying
individual users or posts.
Crucially, moderation happens before ranking. The recommendation pipeline never sees removed
content, which means algorithms cannot accidentally amplify harmful material. Moderation policies are

6.5  Cold-Start Strategy
New users and new creators face the cold-start problem: not enough interaction history to generate
accurate recommendations. Flownet addresses this at three levels:
 Social-graph import: Users who connect their Twitter/X or Instagram accounts bring their interest
signals and follow graph. The system bootstraps recommendations from content their existing follows
engage with on Flownet.
 Content-based filtering: For users with no import, the onboarding wizard collects 3-5 topic
preferences. The initial feed is ranked purely on content similarity (embedding distance to topic
centroids), without requiring collaborative signals.
 Exploration bonus: All built-in algorithms include a configurable exploration factor (default 15%) that
surfaces content outside the user's established preferences, ensuring new creators get distribution
even without an established audience.

7.3  Comparison With Incumbents
Unlike Instagram's 45% cut on sponsored posts (via brand collabs) or YouTube's 55/45 ad-revenue split,
Flownet takes only 5-10% on creator-to-brand transactions and zero on peer-to-peer tipping beyond gas.

8. Tokenomics
Flownet's on-chain economy is designed for utility, not speculation. There is no "FLOW" governance token
at launch — a deliberate decision to avoid the regulatory complexity and speculative distortion that have
plagued Web3 social projects. Instead, the token layer centers on creator tokens: per-creator ERC-20
assets with bonding-curve pricing.
8.1  Creator Token Mechanics
 Bonding curve: Price = k * supplyn, where k and n are set by the creator at mint time. A steeper
curve (higher n) rewards early supporters more aggressively.
 Buy/sell: Anyone can buy or sell creator tokens through the bonding curve contract. Liquidity is
always available — no order book needed.
 Utility: Holding a creator's token unlocks gated content, voting on creator decisions (e.g., next content
topic), and priority in sponsored-placement auctions.
 Fee split: 5% to creator, 2% to protocol, 93% follows the bonding curve.
8.2  Bonding Curve Mathematics
The price of a creator token is determined by a polynomial bonding curve. The spot price at any supply
level is given by:
 P(s) = k · sn
where s is the current supply, k is a base price constant (default: 0.0001 ETH), and n is the curve
exponent (configurable by the creator, typically 1.5-2.0). The cost to purchase Δs tokens starting from
supply s■ is the integral:
 Cost(s■, Δs) = k / (n+1) · [(s■ + Δs)n+1 − s■n+1]
Example: For a creator with k=0.0001, n=2, and current supply of 1,000 tokens, the spot price is 0.0001 ×
1,000² = 100 ETH. Buying the first 100 tokens of a new creator costs 0.0001/3 × 100³ ≈ 33.33 ETH —
early supporters enter cheaply. This mechanism ensures that a creator's most loyal early community is
financially aligned with their growth.

9. Go-to-Market Strategy
Flownet's go-to-market follows a concentric-circles strategy: start with a tight community, prove the
thesis, then expand.
9.1  Phase 1: Anchor Creators (Months 1-4)
Recruit 20-30 mid-tier creators across three verticals — food, fitness, and indie music — who are vocal
about platform frustrations. Provide white-glove onboarding: help them set up creator tokens, import their
audience, and publish their first content. These anchor creators become the proof points that attract
organic users.
9.2  Phase 2: Community-Led Growth (Months 5-8)
 Algorithm marketplace launch: Open the marketplace to third-party developers. Run a hackathon
("Build-a-Feed") with prize pool to seed the ecosystem.
 Creator referral program: Existing creators earn token bonuses for onboarding new creators who
cross a 1,000-follower threshold.
 Public launch: Open registration, PR push via creator testimonials, and targeted ads on platforms
where creator frustration is highest (Twitter/X, TikTok).
9.3  Phase 3: Scale (Months 9-12+)
 Mobile apps: iOS and Android via React Native/Expo
 Advertiser SaaS: Self-serve campaign manager for brands
 Protocol partnerships: Integration with Farcaster (cross-posting), Lens (social graph bridge), and
major wallet providers
 International expansion: Localization for top-5 creator markets (US, Brazil, India, Indonesia, Japan)
